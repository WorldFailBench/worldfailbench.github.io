01 / OVERVIEW

Failure is more
than a binary label.

Robot policies are increasingly evaluated inside generated worlds. But matching the final outcome does not establish that a model reproduces the underlying failure.

WorldFailBench compares closed-loop policy rollouts in world models with reference execution in simulators and on a physical robot. It examines observable errors, the stages where execution breaks down, and the scene changes that turn success into failure.

The benchmark spans LIBERO, RoboTwin, and the G1 humanoid. Experiments reveal diagnostic gaps that aggregate success rates can hide.

Outcome agreement does not guarantee
faithful failure understanding.

RQ3

Failure-Boundary Fidelity

How faithfully do world models reproduce changes in policy success under scene perturbations?

OA: agreement in change direction. GE: error between signed success-rate changes. Factors are evaluated separately.

ROBOTWIN · BEST OA FOR EACH FACTOR
68.5%Position
59.0%Orientation
75.8%Size

Scene-change directions are often misidentified

Ref–Ref OA: 100% for position, 95% for orientation, and 100% for size.

ROBOTWIN · SUCCESS-RATE CHANGE95.2% understated

Models underestimate scene sensitivity

400/420 comparisons understate the reference change magnitude: seven systems × 60 pairs. GE spans 0.459–0.719 across model–factor combinations (Ref–Ref: 0.005–0.020).

LIBERO

OA: 0–17.5% (Ref–Ref: 85–95%). Best GE per factor: 0.504–0.517 (Ref–Ref: 0.035–0.080).
